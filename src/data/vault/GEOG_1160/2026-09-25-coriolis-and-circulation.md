# Lecture Notes: Global Atmospheric Circulation

- Air accelerates from high to low pressure driven by the horizontal Pressure Gradient Force (PGF).
- Earth's rotation introduces the Coriolis force:
  - Deflects moving parcels to the **right** in the Northern Hemisphere.
  - Deflects to the **left** in the Southern Hemisphere.
  - Value is 0 at the equator, maximum at the poles.
- Geostrophic wind develops when PGF and Coriolis achieve dynamic equilibrium in upper-troposphere flow.
- Hadley Cells:
  - Strong solar insolation at ITCZ creates thermal low pressure and towering thunderstorms.
  - Air ascends, cools adiabatically, spreads poleward, and descends at ~30° latitude.
  - The descending dry air limb creates the global subtropical high pressure belts and major arid deserts (e.g. Albuquerque basin, Chihuahuan desert, Sahara).
